## 01. 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes **3,900 customer records** to understand purchasing behaviour, customer segments, product performance, discounts, subscriptions, ratings, shipping preferences, payment methods, and purchase frequency.

The project follows an end-to-end analytics workflow:

**Raw Data → Python EDA & Cleaning → Feature Engineering → PostgreSQL/SQL Analysis → Power BI Dashboard → Insights → Business Recommendations**

The objective is to move beyond descriptive reporting and use customer data to identify commercially relevant patterns that can support **customer retention, product strategy, promotional decisions, and targeted marketing**.

---

## 02. 🎯 Business Problem

The business wants to better understand how customers behave across demographics, products, discounts, reviews, subscriptions, payment methods, shipping preferences, and purchasing frequency.

### Key Business Questions

- Which customer segments contribute the most revenue?
- Which product categories perform best?
- How does discount usage relate to customer spending?
- Which customers represent loyalty and retention opportunities?
- How do subscription, payment, and shipping preferences vary across customers?
- Which products receive the strongest customer ratings?
- How can customer behaviour be translated into targeted business actions?

## 10. 🔍 Key Findings & Business Implications

### 1. Clothing Is the Strongest Revenue Category

**Finding:** Clothing contributes approximately **$100K**, making it the strongest-performing category in the dashboard.

**Business implication:** Clothing is a major contributor to overall revenue, so changes in its demand or availability can have a meaningful effect on total sales.

---

### 2. Young Adults Lead Revenue Contribution

**Finding:** Young Adults contribute approximately **$62K**, the highest revenue contribution among the analyzed age groups.

**Business implication:** This segment currently represents the strongest revenue-generating age group and may warrant deeper analysis of its purchasing behaviour.

---

### 3. Subscription Penetration Is Low

**Finding:** Only **27% of customers are subscribers**, while approximately 73% are non-subscribers.

**Business implication:** A large proportion of the existing customer base remains outside the subscription program, creating a potential retention and conversion opportunity.

---

### 4. Discount Usage Can Be Evaluated Alongside Spending

**Finding:** SQL analysis identifies customers who used discounts while still spending above the average purchase amount.

**Business implication:** Discounting does not necessarily imply low-value purchasing, making customer-level promotional analysis more useful than evaluating discounts only at an aggregate level.

---

### 5. Customer Loyalty Can Be Segmented

**Finding:** Customers were classified as **New, Returning, and Loyal** using previous purchase behaviour.

**Business implication:** Customers at different stages of the relationship may respond differently to acquisition, retention, and loyalty strategies.

---

## 11. 💼 Business Recommendations

### 1. Increase Subscription Conversion

**Finding:** Only **27% of customers are subscribers**.

**Recommended action:** Identify high-frequency and returning non-subscribers and test targeted subscription offers or benefits designed around their purchasing behaviour.

**Expected business value:** A targeted approach can help focus subscription acquisition efforts on customers who already demonstrate stronger engagement.

---

### 2. Prioritize High-Performing Product Categories

**Finding:** Clothing contributes approximately **$100K** in revenue.

**Recommended action:** Monitor inventory availability and evaluate targeted promotions or product expansion within high-performing categories while tracking incremental revenue.

**Expected business value:** Protecting strong-performing categories can help maintain their contribution to overall sales.

---

### 3. Develop Segment-Specific Strategies for Young Adults

**Finding:** Young Adults generate approximately **$62K**, the highest revenue contribution among age groups.

**Recommended action:** Analyze their product preferences, purchase frequency, and discount behaviour to build more targeted campaigns and product recommendations.

**Expected business value:** More relevant offers may improve engagement and repeat purchasing within a high-value customer segment.

---

### 4. Use Discounts More Strategically

**Finding:** Some discounted customers still spend above the average purchase amount.

**Recommended action:** Segment customers by purchase value and frequency before applying promotions, rather than using broad discounts across the entire customer base.

**Expected business value:** This provides a basis for more targeted promotional testing and better control of discount exposure.

---

### 5. Differentiate Retention Strategies by Customer Type

**Finding:** Customers can be segmented into New, Returning, and Loyal groups using previous purchase behaviour.

**Recommended action:** Build separate engagement strategies for each segment, with acquisition-focused messaging for new customers and retention/loyalty initiatives for returning and loyal customers.

**Expected business value:** Segment-specific engagement can make retention efforts more relevant to customer lifecycle stage.

> These recommendations are analytical implications of the project findings rather than measured business outcomes.

## 12. 🧩 Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge 1 — Preparing Raw Data for Multiple Analytical Stages

The dataset needed to support both SQL analysis and Power BI reporting.

**Solution:** Used Python and Pandas to clean, standardize, inspect, and engineer the dataset before loading the prepared data into PostgreSQL and Power BI.

---

### Challenge 2 — Converting Categorical Behaviour Into Analytical Features

Fields such as age and purchase frequency are categorical and difficult to compare directly.

**Solution:** Created meaningful age segments and numerical purchase-frequency intervals to enable clearer segmentation and frequency-based analysis.

---

### Challenge 3 — Connecting Business Questions Across Different Tools

The project required consistency between Python preparation, PostgreSQL analysis, and Power BI reporting.

**Solution:** Followed a structured workflow in which the cleaned dataset became the common analytical foundation for SQL queries and the final dashboard.
